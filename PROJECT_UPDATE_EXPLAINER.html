Block 1 • The Ear of the System

Audio Capture & Signal Quality Conditioning

💡 Concept: "Don't record garbage"
In Plain English

Before we can do any math on a voice, we have to record it through the microphone. But microphones in a dining hall pick up chair rattles, air conditioners, and people whispering. Block 1 asks the student to remain silent for the first 0.5 seconds so it can measure the background noise of the room. It then records the student, checks whether they spoke loud enough, and rejects breath pops or silences before anything else runs.

📁 MATLAB Files in this Block
capture_voice_features.m record_audio_dsp.m assess_recording_quality.m interruptible_pause.m

What they do: capture_voice_features.m manages the 3-2-1 visual countdown and hardware recording. assess_recording_quality.m calculates signal RMS and checks for clipping. interruptible_pause.m (extracted in v4.1.1) handles non-blocking sleep slices so the "Instant Stop" button can immediately abort recordings.

📐 DSP Theory & Formulas Used
1. Nyquist-Shannon Sampling Theorem
Sampled at \(f_s = 8000\text{ Hz}\) (16-bit PCM). By Nyquist, \(f_s \ge 2 f_{\max}\). Human speech formants are intelligible up to 4 kHz. 8 kHz is the international telephony standard and avoids huge matrix sizes.
2. Root Mean Square (RMS) Level Floor
Computes overall signal energy:
RMS = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N} x^2[n]} \ge 0.015
If RMS is below 0.015, the student was too quiet or spoke off-mic.
3. Low-Frequency Rumble Ratio
Measures energy below 120 Hz relative to total energy. Catches wind, desk thumps, or 50 Hz power hum without rejecting genuine speech.
Block 2 • The Filter & Polisher

Audio Preprocessing, Resampling & Noise Cleaning

💡 Concept: "Cleaning dirty glasses before looking"
In Plain English

Raw recordings are imperfect: some students speak quietly, some speak loudly, and background fans create a constant hum. Block 2 subtracts the steady background noise spectrum from the speech, boosts high-frequency vowels that naturally sound faint, and normalizes the volume so a quiet student and a loud student have identical energy levels.

📁 MATLAB Files in this Block
preprocess_audio.m preprocess_template_audio.m spectral_subtract_noise.m agc_normalize.m rational_resample_audio.m

What they do: spectral_subtract_noise.m performs noise reduction. agc_normalize.m standardizes signal gain. rational_resample_audio.m cleanly handles rate conversion (e.g. 44.1 kHz to 8 kHz) using polyphase filtering.

📐 DSP Theory & Formulas Used
1. Spectral Subtraction (Boll's Algorithm)
Using the 0.5s pre-roll noise estimate \(\hat{N}(\omega)\), we subtract background noise power in the frequency domain:
|\hat{S}(\omega)|^2 = \max\left(|Y(\omega)|^2 - \alpha |\hat{N}(\omega)|^2,\; \beta |Y(\omega)|^2\right)
\(\alpha \approx 1.0\) is oversubtraction, \(\beta \approx 0.02\) is the spectral floor to eliminate musical noise.
2. Pre-Emphasis FIR Filter
Human speech naturally rolls off at \(-6\text{ dB/octave}\) due to glottal pulse damping. A 1st-order high-pass FIR filter flattens the spectrum:
H(z) = 1 - 0.97 z^{-1} \quad\iff\quad y[n] = x[n] - 0.97 x[n-1]
3. Automatic Gain Control (AGC)
Scales signal to target RMS (0.12) without amplifying background silence when speech is absent.
Block 3 • The Acoustic Fingerprint

Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC)

💡 Concept: "Extracting the vocal tract shape, ignoring pitch"
In Plain English

You cannot compare two voices by comparing raw squiggly waveforms because the exact waves change every millisecond. Instead, we convert the sound into a biological representation of how the human throat and mouth shape sound. MFCC mimics how the human inner ear (the cochlea) hears frequencies, producing a concise matrix of numbers representing vocal tract resonance.

📁 MATLAB Files in this Block
extract_features.m extract_mfcc_dsp.m extract_dft_features.m select_feature_frontend.m

What they do: extract_mfcc_dsp.m implements the full 5-step MFCC mathematical pipeline from scratch. extract_dft_features.m provides an alternate DFT front-end. select_feature_frontend.m switches between them with calibrated thresholds.

📐 The 5-Step MFCC Mathematical Pipeline
Step 1: Framing & Hamming Windowing
Speech is non-stationary over seconds, but quasi-stationary over 25 ms. Window: \(w[n] = 0.54 - 0.46\cos(2\pi n / N)\) prevents spectral leakage.
Step 2: Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT)
Converts each 25 ms frame to power spectrum \(|X(k)|^2\) via 512-point FFT.
Step 3: Mel-Scale Triangular Filterbank
Human pitch perception is logarithmic above 1 kHz. We map Hz to Mel:
m = 2595 \log_{10}\left(1 + \frac{f}{700}\right)
Step 4: Log Energy Compression
Logarithm of filter energies matches human loudness sensitivity (Weber-Fechner Law).
Step 5: Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT-II)
Converts log-filterbank energies into 13 decorrelated cepstral coefficients, separating vocal tract envelope from fine pitch harmonics.
Block 4 • The Time-Elastic Matcher

Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) Distance Alignment

💡 Concept: "Matching speech even if spoken fast or slow"
In Plain English

If you say your Student ID "2-2-0-6-1-4-7" quickly in 1.5 seconds on Monday, but slowly in 2.2 seconds on Tuesday, a standard point-by-point comparison fails completely. Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) acts like an elastic band: it warps the time axis non-linearly to align matching phonemes (digits) and measures the true acoustic distance between the two recordings.

📁 MATLAB Files in this Block
dtw_distance_dsp.m find_best_voice_match.m voice_match_scores.m xcorr_distance_dsp.m

What they do: dtw_distance_dsp.m computes the minimum-cost warping path between feature matrices. find_best_voice_match.m scans enrolled student templates in Train/ID/ and finds the top winner and runner-up candidate with margin ratios.

📐 DTW Dynamic Programming Formulation
Local Euclidean Cost Matrix
For test frame \(i\) and template frame \(j\), Euclidean distance is:
d(i, j) = \| \mathbf{c}_{test}[i] - \mathbf{c}_{template}[j] \|_2
Global Cumulative Cost Recurrence
Dynamic programming finds the optimal alignment path:
D(i, j) = d(i, j) + \min\begin{cases} D(i-1, j) & \text{(time expansion)} \\ D(i, j-1) & \text{(time compression)} \\ D(i-1, j-1) & \text{(diagonal match)} \end{cases}
Confidence Margin Ratio
\text{Margin} = \frac{d_{\text{runner-up}}}{d_{\text{best}}} \ge 1.20
Ensures the winner is distinctly separated from impostors and other enrolled students.
Block 5 • The Brain & Security Gates

The 5-Gate Decision Engine & Smart Disambiguation

💡 Concept: "5 strict checkpoints before food is served"
In Plain English & v4.1.1 Upgrade

A biometric system must never serve meals based on voice alone: what if it's outside dining hours? What if the student already ate? What if two students have acoustically similar IDs (like 2206147 and 2206148)? In v4.1.1, we fixed the "infinite retry loop": if your spoken ID distinctly beats all other students, you get access automatically on ID alone (no name prompt). Only if two students have a close/ambiguous match does it ask for spoken full name to resolve the tie!

📁 MATLAB Files in this Block
verify_meal_workflow.m student_id_contract.m student_profile.m check_liveness_lowfreq.m meal_window_now.m

What they do: verify_meal_workflow.m coordinates the full live verification pipeline. student_id_contract.m strictly enforces 7-digit ID formatting. meal_window_now.m checks active hall dining service hours.

🛡️ The 5 Sequential Security Gates
Gate 1: Signal Quality & Liveness
Verifies non-zero duration, sufficient speech RMS, and checks low-frequency acoustics to reject speaker-phone playback attacks.
Gate 2: Spoken Student ID Biometric Match
Matches spoken ID against enrolled templates (\(d_{\text{best}} \le 37.16\)). Computes voice similarity percentage (e.g. 88% match).
Gate 3: Distinction vs. Name Disambiguation (v4.1.1)
• Significant Match: Margin \(\ge 1.20\) → Grants access automatically! Zero name prompts.
• Two-Student Ambiguity: Margin \(< 1.20\) → Prompts for spoken full name to disambiguate between the two close candidates.
Gate 4: Meal Window & Sit-out Check
Prevents a student from taking two meals in the same sitting (Breakfast, Lunch, or Dinner).
Gate 5: Monthly Entitlement Gate
Checks MonthlyFeeEntitlement.csv. If dining fee is paid, bypasses token requirement immediately.
Block 6 • The Vault & Records

Meal Logging, Entitlement & Admin Reset

💡 Concept: "The accountant who never forgets"
In Plain English & v4.1.3 Upgrade

Every successful meal serving is written to MealLog.csv (the Excel sheet). It stores the student ID, date, time, meal type, and biometric scores for auditing. In v4.1.3, we added the "Reset meal log..." button in the Admin tab. Operators can wipe today's entries with one click (so students can test again today) or clear the entire log, while keeping enrolled student voice profiles 100% safe and untouched!

What they do: log_meal_csv.m appends verified transactions and enforces one-meal-per-window rules. reset_meal_log_csv.m (new in v4.1.3) cleans today's entries or wipes the sheet cleanly without corrupting column headers.

📊 Database Tables (Single Source of Truth)

Stores StudentID, StudentName, Date, Time, Meal, Method, NameDistance, CouponDistance, Margin. Re-read on every transaction for audit integrity.

Tracks monthly dining fee payments (e.g. 2026-09) by student ID. Bypasses manual coupon codes for paid students.
3. MonthlyCouponRegistry.csv
Fallback 6-digit coupon token registry assigned by hall administrators.

📈 Biometric Metrics (EEE 312 CO4/CO5)
