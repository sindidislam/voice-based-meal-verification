⚙️ Complex Engineering Problem Solving (WP1–WP7) & Activities (EA1–EA5)

The university hall dining hall verification challenge satisfies all criteria of an ABET/IEB Complex Engineering Problem:

Wide Range of Conflicting Requirements (WP1)

High noise suppression is required due to loud mess ambient sounds ($45–65\text{ dB SPL}$), yet aggressive spectral filtering risks distorting crucial formant frequencies ($F_1, F_2$) required to distinguish phonemes. Real-time operation requires $< 200\text{ ms}$ latency on low-cost university PCs while maintaining high template discrimination.

Depth of Knowledge Required (WP2, K1–K5)

Cannot be resolved by trivial heuristics. Requires multirate sampling theory (Oppenheim Ch. 4), discrete-time Fourier analysis (Proakis Ch. 11), psychophysical hearing models (Mel scale), and dynamic programming algorithms.

Societal, Health & Hygiene Impact (WP3, WP5)

Traditional tactile methods (fingerprint pads, RFID cards, paper meal chits) suffer from severe hygiene deficits during peak dining rush hours and are prone to proxy fraud. The touchless acoustic voice interface addresses public health hygiene directly.

Non-Obvious Engineering Synthesis (WP4)

Speech length varies non-linearly between utterances. Simple Euclidean distance or template matching fails completely. Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) with Sakoe-Chiba band constraints was synthesized to resolve temporal variance.

📐 Theoretical Formulations & Engineering Justifications

All signal processing operations adhere strictly to classical discrete-time linear systems theory as documented in Discrete-Time Signal Processing by Alan V. Oppenheim and Digital Signal Processing by John G. Proakis.

1. Rational Multirate Resampling & Automatic Gain Control (AGC)

Microphone inputs are digitized by operating system hardware at $f_{s1} = 44.1\text{ kHz}$. Speech information required for speaker identity is bandlimited below $4\text{ kHz}$. Transmitting 44.1 kHz arrays increases computational complexity by over 500%.

Rational Ratio: L / M = 80 / 441 → 44100 Hz × (80 / 441) = 8000 Hz
Filter Specification: 17,641-tap Kaiser-windowed FIR Lowpass Filter
Cutoff Frequency: f_c = 3600 Hz | Stopband Rejection: > 60 dB

Automatic Gain Control (AGC): Student volume varies based on posture and distance. The system computes signal RMS and normalizes to a calibrated target of $0.0500$:

RMS_actual = √( (1/N) ∑_{n=1}^N x²[n] )
x_norm[n] = x[n] • (0.0500 / max(RMS_actual, 1.0×10&supmin;&sup5;))

Engineering Rationale: If $RMS_{actual} < 10^{-5}$, the frame contains pure silence; the gain limiter refuses amplification to prevent scaling microphone floor noise.

2. Stationary Spectral Noise Subtraction (Boll's Algorithm)

Acoustic canteen noise (fans, distant chatter, refrigeration) is stationary over short intervals ($0.5\text{ s}$). During the initial 0.5 s silent lead-in, the noise power spectrum $\mu_{\text{noise}}(k)$ is estimated across $M_{\text{lead}}$ Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) frames:

μ_noise(k) = (1 / M_lead) ∑_{m=1}^{M_lead} |X_lead(m, k)|²
|S_hat(m, k)| = max( |Y(m, k)| - α • √(μ_noise(k)), β • |Y(m, k)| )

Where $\alpha = 2.0$ is the over-subtraction factor designed to remove noise peaks, and $\beta = 0.02$ is the spectral flooring parameter preventing mathematical negative energy and suppressing artificial "musical noise". Empirical error power decreases from $0.02983$ to $0.01741$ (a 42% reduction).

3. Hybrid Short-Time Energy (STE) & Zero Crossing Rate (ZCR) Endpointing

Accurate temporal segmentation separates true speech from breathing and room silence:

STE: E[m] = ∑_{n} (x[n] • w[m - n])²
ZCR: Z[m] = 0.5 ∑_{n} | sgn(x[n]) - sgn(x[n - 1]) | • w[m - n]

A dual-threshold state machine detects speech onset when $E[m] > T_{\text{high}}$ (high energy vowels) and expands boundaries backward and forward to $T_{\text{low}}$ based on high zero-crossing rates characteristic of unvoiced consonants (/s/, /t/, /k/).

4. Low-Frequency Liveness Ratio (Anti-Spoofing Physical Biometrics)

To prevent synthetic playback attacks (e.g. playing a pre-recorded WAV file from a smartphone loudspeaker), the system exploits the fundamental physics of miniature acoustic transducers. Human male vocal cords vibrate at $85–180\text{ Hz}$ and female cords at $165–255\text{ Hz}$, transmitting substantial sub-200 Hz energy through chest resonance. In contrast, smartphone micro-speakers have steep acoustic high-pass filters rolling off sharply below $250–300\text{ Hz}$.

Ratio_live = ( ∑_{0 ≤ f ≤ 200 Hz} |X(f)|² ) / ( ∑_{0 ≤ f ≤ 4000 Hz} |X(f)|² )
Rule: If Ratio_live < θ_live → Reject transaction immediately as synthetic loudspeaker replay.

5. 39-Dimensional Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC)

Features are extracted using $25\text{ ms}$ Hamming windows with $10\text{ ms}$ overlap:

  1. FFT power spectrum mapped onto 24 triangular bandpass filters spaced along the non-linear Mel scale:
    Mel(f) = 2595 • log10( 1 + f / 700 )
  2. Logarithmic compression of filterbank energies simulating auditory loudness perception.
  3. Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT-II) decorrelating filter energies into 13 cepstral coefficients ($c_0$ to $c_{12}$).
  4. Append first temporal derivatives ($\Delta$, velocity) and second temporal derivatives ($\Delta\Delta$, acceleration) yielding a robust 39-dimensional feature vector per frame.

6. Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) with Margin Ratio Separation

Speech tempo fluctuates dynamically. DTW calculates the optimal warping path between test matrix $\mathbf{C} \in \mathbb{R}^{39 \times N}$ and template $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{39 \times M}$:

Local Distance: d(i, j) = || c_i - r_j ||_2
Cumulative Cost: γ(i, j) = d(i, j) + min{ γ(i-1, j), γ(i, j-1), γ(i-1, j-1) }
Sakoe-Chiba Band Constraint: | i - j | ≤ R (prevents non-physical temporal stretching)

Dual Decision Metric: A match is accepted only if:
1. Minimum DTW distance satisfies $D_1 \le 37.1585$.
2. Runner-up separation margin satisfies $D_2 / D_1 \ge 1.20$.

🔄 Version 4 Architectural Updates: Single Source of Truth

In response to operational testing of Version 3, Version 4 introduces crucial decoupling of dining fees and enrollment credentials:

Deficiencies in Version 3

  • Redundant Static File Checking: Both verify_meal_workflow.m and manual_entry_workflow.m looked up a static file Train/ID/<id>/code.txt before validating against the monthly registry. If an admin assigned a new month's coupon, the static text file caused desynchronization and false refusals.
  • Illogical Enrollment Requirement: When enrolling a new student's voice, the admin was forced to enter a 6-digit coupon. Since coupons rotate monthly, recording a coupon at voice enrollment created technical confusion.
  • Rigid Audio Take Import: Pre-recorded WAV files could only be imported by pointing to a complete folder, preventing single-take updates.

Version 4 Solutions Deployed

  • Single Source of Truth (Gate 5): All static code.txt file checks have been completely removed. Dynamic monthly coupon tokens are validated solely against MonthlyCouponRegistry.csv via consume_monthly_coupon.
  • Clean ID + Name Voice Enrollment: The coupon edit field and button have been removed from the New Enroll UI. Enrollment captures acoustic profiles for ID and Name only; coupons are assigned dynamically in Admin.
  • Flexible File Picker: Added enrol_import_files allowing multi-selection of individual .wav takes via file dialog alongside existing folder import.
  • Dedicated Regression Suite: Added verify_coupon_registry_gate.m guaranteeing 100% Gate 5 integrity.

📋 Standard Operating Procedures (SOP)

A. Normal User & Student Patron SOP

1

Approach Kiosk & Prepare

Stand 15–30 cm from the microphone. Ensure face mask or scarf does not obstruct vocal sound path.

2

Press "Record and Verify" & Observe 3-2-1 Countdown

Press the button once. Watch the visual countdown (3... 2... 1...). Remain completely silent during the 0.5-second lead-in while the kiosk profiles room noise.

3

Speak 7-Digit Student ID Naturally

When the screen says >>> SPEAK NOW! <<<, speak your seven digits clearly at normal conversational volume (e.g. "Two, Two, Zero, Six, One, Four, Seven").

4

Spoken Name Retry (If Prompted)

If the ID distance is borderline, the kiosk will prompt you to speak your full enrolled name. Follow the same silent lead-in and speak clearly.

5

Monthly Dining Token Entry (First Visit of Calendar Month)

If dining for the first time this month, enter the 6-digit coupon token provided by the administration into the coupon field. For all subsequent meals within 30 days, leave the coupon field empty.

B. System Administrator SOP (Monthly Fee Distribution)

  1. Log in to the Admin tab using authorized credentials (default demo: admin / admin; update in dsp_parameters.m prior to deployment).
  2. Press Unlock / meal log to initiate administrative session.
  3. To issue a token: In Assign coupon, enter the Student ID and enter a unique 6-digit code. Press Assign for current month. Provide this code to the student upon dining fee receipt.
  4. To inspect records: Press Coupon registry to confirm which students have consumed tokens. Press Monthly payments to inspect active 30-day entitlements.
  5. CRITICAL: When leaving the counter, always press Lock to wipe memory caches and remove private workspaces.

🔧 Troubleshooting & Diagnostic Guide

Observed Status / Error Prompt Probable Technical Cause Recommended Corrective Action
REFUSED: Energy too low / empty recording Microphone muted, wrong Windows default audio input, or disconnected jack. Press Test microphone. Check Windows Settings → Sound → Input Device and verify microphone permissions are enabled for MATLAB.
REFUSED: Synthetic replay or low liveness ratio Mobile phone audio playback detected, or patron spoke with severe high-frequency whispering. Instruct patron to speak naturally using authentic vocal fold phonation. Ensure acoustic path is unobstructed.
REFUSED: Voice match distance 42.1 exceeds 37.15 High background cafeteria noise burst during speech, or patron spoke prematurely during 0.5s lead-in. Remind student to wait for the >>> SPEAK NOW! <<< prompt before speaking. If failure repeats, use Manual entry.
REFUSED: Dining service is closed at this time Attempt made outside scheduled meal hours. Verify system clock. Breakfast (07:00–09:30), Lunch (12:00–14:30), Dinner (19:00–21:30). Counter automatically opens when window arrives.

REFUSED: Coupon 000000 is not assigned for 2026-09 Typo in 6-digit coupon token, or token was consumed previously. Admin should verify MonthlyCouponRegistry.csv to confirm student's assigned monthly token.

⚖️ Declaration of Academic Integrity & Biometric Ethics

Adherence to IEEE Code of Ethics & Institutional Policy

The development of this project adheres strictly to Article 1.2 of the IEEE Code of Ethics regarding privacy, data integrity, and technological transparency:

  • Biometric Dignity: Human speech signals are treated as private biometric assets. Voice features are mathematically extracted into cepstral parameters and never uploaded to public commercial clouds.
  • Access Equity: Students with vocal fatigue, speech impediments, or temporary laryngitis are provided an equal, non-discriminatory fallback via the Manual Entry interface with identical dining privileges.
  • Academic Authorship: All mathematical algorithms, software routines, and documentation were implemented by the project team for the EEE 312 Sessional course under BUET academic guidelines.
